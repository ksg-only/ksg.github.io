## A Bike That Was Never Meant for Me

Let me be clear — this was a women's bike. A Schwinn Inspired Trailway in powder blue, with a step-through aluminum frame designed for someone around 5'4". I'm 6'1".

It was my sister's bike from her time at ASU, sitting forgotten in our garage. And in March 2020, when Covid shut everything down — the basketball courts, the campus, the social life I was barely starting to build — it was all I had.

No budget for a new bike. No bike shops open anyway. Just this undersized blue Schwinn with a seat cranked to its absolute max height, my knees still coming up too high on every pedal stroke.

I didn't care. I needed to move.

## The Covid Bike

When you're stuck in a state like Arizona, with nothing but Zoom classes and triple digit weather, something breaks in you if you don't find an outlet. Basketball was gone. Friends were gone. The courts were locked with chains.

So I started riding. 6 AM, before the Arizona sun turned everything into an oven. Just me and this comically small bike rolling through empty streets in Chandler.

The first few rides were brutal — not because of fitness, but because of the bike. My back hunched over a frame too short for my torso. The handlebars that were "ergonomic" for someone a foot shorter than me forced my wrists into awkward angles. The padded saddle? Let's just say it wasn't designed for my sit bones. Every ride ended with numbness in places I won't describe.

## Learning Chandler One Ride at a Time

But here's the thing — none of that mattered after the first mile.

Chandler sits on the southern edge of the Phoenix metro, and every morning I'd pick a direction and just go. The 21-speed Shimano drivetrain was enough to handle the flat desert roads and the occasional overpass climb. The 700c wheels rolled smooth on the paved paths along the canals and through the neighborhoods I'd never explored on foot.

I discovered:
- The emptiness of south Chandler where the suburbs just... end, and desert takes over
- Sunrise hitting the Santan mountains while the city was still asleep
- Canal paths that stretched for miles
- Random parks, murals, and coffee shops I never knew existed

Every morning I pushed a little further. Found a new southernmost point. Clicked a photo of the bike against some new backdrop. It became a ritual — the one thing Covid couldn't take from me.

## The Struggles Were Real

Let me not romanticize this too much. Riding a small-frame bike as a tall dude had real problems:

**The fit was wrong in every way.** My knees nearly hit the handlebars. My lower back screamed after anything over 10 miles. I looked ridiculous — a lanky guy folded onto a blue step-through like a circus act.

**The suspension fork was mushy.** It bobbed with every pedal stroke, eating my energy on the flat stretches where I didn't need any suspension at all.

**The brakes were just okay.** Linear pull brakes in Arizona heat and dust meant I was adjusting them constantly. They worked, but they demanded attention.

**The saddle was torture.** I eventually got used to it, the way you get used to anything. But those first few weeks — brutal.

## The 54-Mile Vaccine Ride
![Schwinn in Tempe Beach Park - March 25, 2021](/assets/images/IMG_1102.jpg)
> March 25, 2021 - Tempe Beach Park

One story captures this whole experience perfectly.

As a teaching assistant at ASU, I qualified for an early Covid vaccine. The catch? All the family members were gone to LA and I was alone in Gilbert, AZ. The vaccination site was at the ASU Tempe campus. Not much but just about 27 miles one way.

I had a car but no driving license. BUT I had the Schwinn.

So I attended my last class at around 2PM and jumped on the Schwinn. 27 miles to Tempe on a frame that had my knees pumping like pistons next to my chest. Got the shot. I still remember that big fat Chipotle burrito I ate after the vaccination. (Probably the best burrito ever) 

Turned around. 27 miles back. 54 miles total on a bike that wasn't meant for rides longer than a trip to the grocery store. On a frame that wasn't meant for my body. In Arizona.

By the time I reached home, I was completely destroyed. Every muscle ached. My back was locked up. My legs felt like concrete. And here's the thing — I genuinely could not tell if it was the vaccine effects or the 54-mile bike ride.

To this day, I have no idea if I had any vaccine side effects. The Schwinn made sure of that.

## Why I Never Wanted Another Bike

Here's what people don't understand when I tell this story. They say, "Why didn't you just buy a proper bike?"

Because it was 2020. Because I was a grad student on a tight budget in a country I'd been in for less than a year. I had barely stopped converting USD into INR. 

It didn't fit me, but it fit my life. It was there when nothing else was. It rolled when I needed it to roll. It took me places I wouldn't have gone otherwise.

I didn't dream of a better bike because I didn't know what better meant yet. I just knew that every morning at 6 AM, this blue Schwinn got me out the door. And that was everything.

But by the time bike shops opened up, this bike had already become *my* bike.

## The Bike Itself

For what it's worth — the Schwinn Trailway is genuinely a solid bike for its intended rider. If you're anyone around 5'4" to 5'6" looking for a comfortable hybrid:

- The step-through frame was practical and easy to mount
- The 21-speed gearing handles flats and moderate hills well
- The ride is comfortable and upright
- It's reliable and low-maintenance
- The bike was fairly nice-looking. 

It just wasn't built for someone like me. But it carried me anyway.

## What It Became

I didn't know it at the time, but those awkward, uncomfortable, beautiful morning rides on a too-small women's bike in Chandler, Arizona were the beginning of everything that followed in my cycling life.

Every cyclist has a first bike. Mine was borrowed, undersized, and powder blue. And I wouldn't trade that story for anything.
